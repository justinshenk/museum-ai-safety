Make AI risk something people can touch.

We're building a pop-up exhibition on AI safety that opens in Berlin this November, and we're looking for hands-on installations, immersive experiences, art, and explainers that let a visitor feel how AI systems work, what's at stake as they get more capable, and where people still have a say. Finished work, work in progress, and bare ideas are all welcome.

01

What we're looking for

Hands-on installation

Let the visitor do the thing

Physical or on-screen installations where the visitor acts and the system responds. Fast to grasp, hard to forget.

  • Try to stop an agent that would rather not be stopped
  • Train a tiny model and watch it learn the wrong lesson
  • Steer a chatbot with a persuasion dial
Immersive

Put the visitor inside the scenario

VR, XR, audio walks, or room-scale pieces of a few minutes, running from a single station with a volunteer attendant.

  • Walk through a data center as an inference request
  • Sit in on an AI incident post-mortem
  • See a city change under three automation forecasts
Art

Make it felt before it is understood

Sculpture, print, sound, video, performance. Work that makes the abstract stakes of AI development perceptible.

  • A physical object that encodes a model's weights
  • Portraits of expert disagreement
  • Sound made from a model's uncertainty
Explainer

One idea, clearly

Posters, short films, zines, or tabletop demos that explain a single concept well: interpretability, oversight, misalignment, the EU AI Act.

  • A timeline of AI development and forecasts
  • What interpretability finds inside a model
  • Who regulates AI in Europe, on one wall

15 m² shared

Roughly four to six works at once. Tabletop and wall pieces are easiest to place.

Outdoor
Unlimited

Weatherproof or tent-covered work welcome. November in Berlin: cold, wet, dark by 16:30.

Mains, basic WiFi

Standard sockets. Bring adapters for anything unusual.

Attendance
Mostly unattended

Volunteers cover the room, not each piece. Headsets get a dedicated attendant.

Duration
2 to 3 days per venue

Language
English, German

Either is fine. Both is better. We can help translate labels.

05

For creators

What you get.

  • Your work in front of over 1,000 people, across the Berlin venues and online, and used in briefings for policymakers and journalists
  • Credit as you choose: full name, alias, or anonymous
  • Photo and video documentation of the installed work
  • Inclusion in the open-source exhibit kit, if you opt in
  • A materials or travel stipend where funding allows, confirmed with the shortlist

You keep your work.

  • Ownership stays with you. We take a non-exclusive license to display it.
  • The default license is CC BY-NC-SA 4.0, or display-only if you prefer.
  • Work is shown at your own risk. The pilot is volunteer-run and cannot insure pieces.
  • Interactive pieces may record anonymous usage data on the device, never personal data.
  • Content must be accurate.

Pitching asks for one consent only. Full terms are shared with the shortlist before stage two.

06

Questions

Does the work have to be finished?

No. Ideas and in-progress work are welcome at stage one. Tell us honestly where it stands. We'll weigh feasibility against the November date.

Is this a competition?

It's an open call, not a contest. There is no ranking or prize, only selection for the space we have. We may highlight a few works in the programme.

Who decides?

A small volunteer committee screens every pitch against four criteria: feasibility, accuracy, engagement, and fit with the exhibition's aims. Advisors from the AI safety field review the shortlist.

Can I submit more than one idea?

Yes. One form entry per idea.

Can I use AI tools to make the work?

Yes, and please disclose it. Works about AI that are partly made with AI can be more interesting for it, as long as the claims are accurate.

What happens to my work afterwards?

Physical works go back to you. Digital works stay in the exhibition's rotation for later venues unless you withdraw. If you opt in, they also join the open-source kit other cities can stage.

Is there a fee or budget?

No fee to submit. The pilot is volunteer-run and currently unfunded, so stipends depend on grants in progress. We'll be straight with you about what we can cover.

Not pitching? Help us build it.

We need screeners, installers, venue hosts, and people to talk to visitors. A few hours a week, no museum experience needed.
